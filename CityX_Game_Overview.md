# CITY X: game overview (for brainstorming)

A summary of the game as of November 2026: what it is, what is already playable, what is planned, and what is still undecided. It is written so
another AI can understand the whole game and suggest ideas. **"Built"** means it is in the game now; **"planned"** means decided but not made;
**"open"** means not decided.

---

## 1. The pitch

**An online megacity where you level forever and get absurdly powerful, and where everything you have can be lost.** You build your own fighting
style from dozens of skill trees, join or start a faction, fight wars, explore a world that goes down to Hell and the void and up to space, and hunt
the one player in the world who holds a god-tier spell.

- **Core loop:** grow, risk it, fight, lose it or take it.
- **The goal of the game is to be the most overpowered.** Time put in should show: a 300-hour veteran should be able to toy with a level-20
  player who found a gun (like RuneScape or WoW, not a shooter where only aim decides).
- **Tone:** eerie and meta as an undertone (TempleOS, "Poorly Planned"), with comedy; not the main point.
- **Genre:** online action RPG / MMO-lite, third-person, PvP and PvE, one shared world.
- **Platform:** Windows PC first (Steam). Controller support before release. Consoles (Xbox with crossplay first) only if the PC version does well.
- **Made by:** one person (a non-programmer designer) with an AI writing the code, in Unity.

## 2. Look and feel

- **Cel-shaded like Jet Set Radio:** flat colour bands and black outlines on everything.
- **Third-person camera like Saints Row: The Third:** over the right shoulder, swappable to the left.
- **The player is 2.6 m tall:** everything in the world is scaled about 1.3 times real size.
- **Characters are drawn by players:** you paint your body, hair and face yourself in the character creator.
- **Models:** the owner's own drawings turned into 3D with an image-to-3D tool, then cleaned up; or built in code.
- **Combat feel:** Dark Souls pacing with combos (owner's choice), full-body animations made for the game rather than bought packs, ragdolls, dismemberment and gore.

## 3. Lore (the owner's)

- **Terraplex 9:** an artificial exoplanet built in the year 30,000, nine times the size of Jupiter.
- **The rapture:** the **Demiurge**, a false god who knows of no god greater than itself, has awoken and begun a great rapture. The endless megacities are mostly abandoned. Spirits, monsters and leftover AI and machinery now fight over this abandoned space station / artificial planet.
- **The players** are **fallen angels** who fell from the Demiurge's domain in the wake of the rapture and forgot their memories. They must regain their strength and decide whether to join the Demiurge or fight for something greater.
- **The Organization:** a mysterious group of artificial superintelligences in human-like artificial bodies.
- **Open:** how karma's angel/demon side ties to the Demiurge; whether Heaven (top of the world) is the Demiurge's domain; the rescuer who brings new players to the hub ("some light entity, maybe god", not final).

## 4. The world

**One endless world**, generated from a seed, so every player sees the same city without it being stored. It is infinite both ways vertically, with each zone very tall (hours to cross). **No digging and no building:** every way down is *found*, behind a gate that takes curiosity or knowledge to open. Difficulty rises the further you go from the surface.

- **Down:** surface (starter city, Cybertown with neon, mossy Ruins, maybe a gothic district) → Sewers → Caves → Pillars → Underground City → Underground Sewer → The Deep → Mantle (lava) → Hell → The Black (zero-gravity void) → The White (endless).
- **Up:** surface → Clouds → Sky City → Stratosphere → Heaven → Space (endless; alien cities spawn by chance).
- **Zone rewards:** zone-exclusive items, and rare **skill trees found in zones** (read a tome).
- **Boss dungeons:** fixed ones set by the seed, plus rare timed event dungeons announced to the whole server.
- **Waypoints:** finding a zone's way down once makes it a waypoint you can return to.

**Built:**
- The surface megacity: streets, highways, layered levels in the style of Chongqing (footbridges, stacked streets), American-style streets, traffic with cars, civilians, parks and open spaces.
- Building exteriors and some interiors, with breakable glass, doors and windows, light switches, room lights, readable books, benches, a radio.
- Weather (rain, snow, wet streets) and day and night lighting.
- **The Sewers' first build:** tunnels under every road, rooms, manhole ladders, tunnel mouths under bridges, murky water, pitch darkness lit only by torches, grey brick.

**Sewers (planned):** manholes hard to open (explosives or Strength), hidden gated arches, flood tunnels; camps and a deeper level later.

## 5. The sky hub (partly built, design changing)

A safe social city floating at cloud height: no PvP and no damage there. You trade, socialise, recruit for factions, use the market board and train skills. You respawn there after death. You leave by jumping off the edge with a parachute.

- **Built:** a round island at cloud height, with a spiral staircase that is being removed.
- **Planned (owner):** a **futuristic floating space station** the size of a WoW capital city (Stormwind / Orgrimmar).
- **Getting up:** no staircase. A **port on the ground** with a **flying ferry** every ~15 minutes, carrying as many players as fit, like WoW's boats. The guards are at the port.
- **Port campers:** attacking at the port puts a **bounty** on you, shown on everyone's map, lasting until you die (it survives logging out).
- **Removing a bounty:** a rare NPC, the **Smuggler** (inspired by Breaking Bad's "disappearer"), gives you a new identity. You pay, and he hands you a random generic name ("Stan Johnson"), so veterans learn to spot people who have used him.
- **Crowd goal:** hundreds of players at once.
- **Ideas still being worked on:** parkour, a duelling arena, player shops.

## 6. The tutorial (first draft built)

About 10 minutes, and starting the game throws you straight in.

1. You fall from the sky (the **conveyor-belt-in-the-sky** character intro).
2. You meet **Old Guy**, who asks you to help clear out his house.
3. You find starter gear: the **Kitchen Knife, Magic Wand and Slingshot**, with the Butter Knife and Baseball Bat hidden.
4. You fight an intruder and turn the power back on with the fuse box.
5. You learn stats, skills and spells.
6. He suggests starting a faction together.
7. Comedic ending: just as he says "alright I think we're safe now", bullets rain through the windows and kill you both. You wake in Heaven or Hell, and a rescuer brings you to the hub.

Other rules:
- You can walk away at any time and you're dropped into the real game.
- Karma choices in it: spare or finish a begging enemy, open the boxes in the basement you were told not to touch.
- Old Guy's house exists in the real city too, abandoned and claimable, right by the hub's drop-off (dangerous).

## 7. Getting around

**Built:**
- Running, sprinting, vaulting, climbing walls and ledges, wall stick, super jump, dash, crouch, ladders.
- Grappling hook with rope swinging, jetpack, angel wings (flight), parachute.
- Sitting on benches; cars in traffic.

**Planned ways home:**
- **Teleport crystals:** found, fairly common; 10 s charge; blocked after fights and in enemy territory. They take you home, to the hub, or to a player who accepts.
- **A Magic Mirror-like item** (super rare) that takes you home.
- **Portal spells** (Wizard tree): allies go through first, the portal closes behind you; only high-level players get them.
- **A Portal Gun** (Rick and Morty style) with a ~10 hour cooldown.
- **"Finding your way back home after a long journey is part of the game."**

## 8. Characters, stats and skills

**Creator (built):**
- Paint your body freely: brush, bucket, colours, hex codes.
- Hair styles, beards, drawn face parts.
- Up to 36 characters, picked from a **conveyor belt in the sky**.
- Clothes are separate items you wear.

**Stats (built):**
- Infinite levels; XP needed is 100 × level; 5 stat points per level.
- **24 stats:** Max Health, Health Regen, Max Stamina, Stamina Regen, Max Mana, Mana Regen, Melee Damage, Melee Speed, Firearm Damage, Reload Speed, Fire Rate, Spell Power, Cast Speed, Cooldown Reduction, Armor Penetration, Accuracy, Recoil Control, Movement Speed, Flight Speed, Vehicle Handling, Armor, Magic Resistance, Stability, Block Chance.
- Percentages never reach 100%. Speed has a ceiling, because the city is built around you as you move.

**Skill trees:**
- **Classless** (like Terraria). Your **title** comes from your top one or two trees: all fire = Pyromancer, fire + sword = Flameblade.
- **Three groups:** **Warrior** (melee styles), **Ranger** (ranged: archery, throwing, guns, explosives; looking for a better name), **Wizard** (base magic and spells with no element) plus one tree per element.
- **Element trees (draft):** Pyromancer, Cryomancer, Stormcaller, Tidecaller, Geomancer, Aeromancer, Voidwalker (gravity), Necromancer, Plaguebringer, Hemomancer (blood), Lightbringer.
- **Weapon styles (draft):** Brawler, One-Handed Melee, Two-Handed Melee, Handguns, Long Guns, Explosives, Archery and Throwing, Magic Weapons.
- **Freerunner (movement):** vault for everyone, then Dash, Climb and Super Jump paths.
- **Kinds of skill:** passive, ability (stamina), spell (mana), stance/transformation, summon, modifier ("Fireball splits into three"), god tier.
- **Infinite upgrades:** every rank costs 10% more and gives 10% more, so numbers reach millions late game.
- **Variety comes from:** a limited hotbar, modifiers and **element reactions** (wet + lightning = shock, frozen + heavy hit = shatter, poison gas + fire = explosion).
- **Built:** a skill map like Skyrim's constellations (8 trees for now), skill points, a hotbar on keys 1–0, and these spells: Fireball, Sword Spin, Ice Shards, Poison Breath, Acid Rain, Octaslash, Sword Wave, Bloodrage, Glacier Spikes, Arcane Orb, Arcane Barrier, Grow and Shrink. Plus a Fire Enhancement enchant: your next hit sets things alight.

**Hybrids are expected:** most players will mix, say, a sword, magic and a gun. Every player has **two loadouts: primary and secondary weapon.**

## 9. Combat

**Built:**
- **Dark Souls-style melee with combos:** light chains, held heavies, running attacks, guard, parry, dash, stamina, poise and stagger; you move freely while attacking.
- Ragdolls with getting back up, **dismemberment** and gore, damage numbers, hit-stop.
- Bullets can be deflected by a sword guard.
- Armour in three weights (no slowdown, owner's rule).
- Loot what enemies wear (Skyrim style).
- A playtest bot that fights real duels.

**Damage types:** melee hits are **Blade (maybe "Slash"), Blunt or Pierce**, set per move. Hybrid weapons split hits 50/50. Guns are **Bullet**, bows **Bolt**, plus Impact, Explosive and the elements.

**Balance rules (owner):**
- Guns are powerful and rare; a gunner beats a melee player at low level, but melee builds get bullet-blocking and gap-closing tools.
- Ammo drops from gun enemies and is sold by NPCs, never random.
- Some weapons are meant to be overpowered and as hard to get as they are strong.
- Fights should last long enough to use your hotbar and strategies.
- No annoying penalties: no carry weight, no armour slowdown.

**Weapons built:**
- **Unarmed:** fists.
- **One-handed melee:** Combat Sword, Machete, Kitchen Knife (backstab), Butter Knife, Metal Crowbar, glass bottles (break, then throw).
- **Two-handed melee:** Spike Sword (greatsword), Baseball Bat, Sledgehammer, Bo Staff (metal pole), Wood Plank, Spear (may be cut).
- **Other melee:** a heat-glowing Chain (inFamous-style).
- **Guns:** Glock (and Dual Pistols as a skill), AK-47, pump shotgun, RPG.
- **Strung and thrown:** bow, crossbow, slingshot; Wooden Boomerang, Giant Boomerang, Iron Spiked Giant Boomerang (carried on the back).
- **Explosives:** frag grenade, Semtex.
- **Magic:** magic wand and magic staff; staffs of Fireballs, Ice Shards, Water, Flame, Frost, Arcane Beam, Healing; the Wraith Staff (summons a Wraith).
- **Tools and others:** flamethrower, jerry can + lighter, grappling hook, riot shield, round shield, torch, flashlight, spray can (graffiti).

**Weapons planned:** nail bat and wire bat, golf club, baton, axes, scythes (up to a Demon Scythe), Deagle, Dragunov, Barrett, minigun, laser guns and swords, RPG minigun, Charged Blade (electric sword), back-carried greatswords and big guns, a throwables branch.

**Consumables:**
- **PHIZZ** (in-game banana soda brand): can heals 80 over 10 s, full bottle 100 over 10 s.
- Drinks stack, can go on the hotbar, and are drunk with an animation.
- They can be thrown: they splash and leave a stain, and the empty bottle becomes a weapon.

## 10. Death and karma (the hardcore heart)

**Death (owner's direction):**
- When a character dies you make a new one, then must reach **Heaven or Hell** (depending on karma) to **rescue your dead character**.
- Heaven has no enemies; Hell is hard.
- Only you can rescue your own soul; friends and factions can escort you.
- A rescued character returns with its level, skills and stats, but the items it carried were dropped where it died.
- Souls wait forever (no timers, "not mobile-game gimmicks").
- **Not settled:** an older plan (drop items, reset level, keep skills; hardcore permadeath worlds) and how war deaths count.

**Karma (owner's):**
- You start white (no notoriety).
- Killing people who weren't fighting back pushes you toward evil.
- **Up to halfway:** NPCs like you less and trade worse.
- **Past halfway:** NPCs and hobos attack on sight.
- **Fully evil:** evil NPCs give you evil quests, and some evil NPCs aren't hostile to you.

Draft ideas still on the table:
- A name colour going from white to red.
- Karma fades with time played.
- Reds are banned from the hub, shown on the map and carry bounties.
- An outlaw way to play: hideouts, a black market.

**Spawn guards (owner):** fight near spawn and the guards come after you; anger them enough and **they raid your base to kill you** (no offline raids; they come for you, not your stuff).

## 11. Factions and territory

Minecraft Factions-style, in the city.

- **Relations:** ally, truce, neutral, enemy.
- **Territory grows:** a room (place a bed), then a whole building (cleared of enemies and with power on), then blocks and districts.
- **Furniture:** bring it home to decorate; a couch is dragged, or carried with a friend.
- **Faction power** limits how much land you hold.
- **War:** declare, 24 h warm-up, a goal, then surrender, truce or exhaustion.
- **No offline raids:** sieges only in windows the defenders choose. Battles are capped per side to start (50 vs 50).
- **Faction emblem:** drawn by players.
- **Solo players:** can still get strong ("pretty auraful, like Kirito").
- **Base defences planned:** turrets, traps, maybe NPC guards.
- **Recruits:** joining a good faction lets low-level players get gear and be carried into higher zones.

**NPC followers (planned):**
- Befriend NPCs by doing their quests, then bring them home: one per bed, about 4 beds a room.
- They sell or trade things, like healing items and ammunition.
- They are **not fighters**, because the owner doesn't want followers in the PvP meta. They do fight back if attacked or if the base is attacked.
- No upkeep.

## 12. Enemies and NPCs

**Built:**
- The **Wraith**: a low-level ghost made of smoke.
- **Crimson Claw**: a monster boss.
- Testers: a swordsman, the "Shoota" gunner, Crazy Guy.
- Enemies spawn at random like Minecraft: monsters and ghosts outside, humanoids indoors. Cleared buildings stay empty a day.
- Enemies wander and are synced between players. Civilians walk the streets.

**Plan:**
- Most enemies are humanoid: look + loadout + stats + one of ~6 brains (brawler, duelist, caster, gunner, summoner, ambusher).
- Non-human monsters are for bosses and each zone's signature creature.

**Planned NPCs:**
- **Hobos:** many looks, random outfits, mostly weak weapons and sometimes good ones. Neutral until hit; others join in, then flee from guns. About 15% are brave. They remember evil players.
- **Aimless Wanderer:** roams with better weapons, trades, has no quests ("if I had any quests I'd just do them myself"), and is a tough fight.
- **The Smuggler:** sells you a new identity.

## 13. Loot, items and economy

- **Loot is first come, first served:** one stock per building, refilled at random about daily, like Rust. Claimed buildings never refill.
- **Destroyed buildings** are repaired by robots the corporations send down (a chance to fight them).
- **Rarity tiers:** Common (grey), Uncommon (green), Rare (blue), Very Rare (purple), Legendary, Godly.
- **Item cards:** look at an item to see its name; inspect it for a card with stats compared against what you hold. No durability.
- **Clothes built:** T-shirts (several), Midnight Coat, Fur Hooded Parka (green, maroon), jeans (black, blue, bred, maroon), khakis, sneakers (white, black and white, black), Farmer Hat, Knight Helmet, 3D glasses, fingerless gloves.
- **Not designed yet:** the currency system and the player market.

## 14. God-tier spells

- **Only one of each in the entire game.** Examples: a flood, meteors, an Almighty Push, a zombie horde, a giant transformation.
- **"The players become the bosses":** once the top bosses are beaten, the endgame threat is other players with boss-tier power.
- Won at an event, not ground out. **Lost on death:** the killer takes it if they qualify. It returns to the world if the holder is offline too long.
- The holder is shown on the map, and a server-wide announcement goes out. Factions protect their holder.
- The city heals after destruction.

## 15. Social and multiplayer

- **One world for everyone**, official servers only, with servers placed for America and the West.
- **Built:** online play (host and join), proximity voice chat (muffled through walls), server-side character saving, emotes (/pushups, dancing), graffiti with a spray can, and other players seeing everything you do.
- **Planned:** text chat as proximity (maybe a global channel), faction radio, uncensored chat with a personal filter, report, mute and block. **18+.**

## 17. Open questions (good places for ideas)

- **Currency and economy:** what money is, how players earn and spend it, the player market, trading.
- **Quests:** what NPC quests look like in a procedural endless city; befriending NPCs; quest givers.
- **Each zone's identity:** signature enemies, bosses, puzzles and gates for the Sewers, Caves, Pillars, Underground City, The Deep, Mantle, Hell, The Black, The White, Clouds, Sky City, Stratosphere, Heaven and Space.
- **Boss dungeons** and server-wide event dungeons.
- **God-tier spell events:** how each is won and the world event around it.
- **The death and rescue loop:** what the journey to Heaven or Hell is like, and how the soul is found and freed.
- **Karma:** evil quests, outlaw factions, what good karma unlocks.
- **Factions:** reasons to hold land, base defences, war goals, rankings.
- **NPC followers:** which NPCs, what each offers, their quests.
- **Skill tree specifics:** signature abilities and modifiers for each element and weapon style; hybrid class names; element reactions.
- **Weapons and items** for every rarity tier, especially Legendary and Godly.
- **The sky hub space station:** districts, activities, the ferry and port, the guards' design.
- **Lore:** the Demiurge, the Organization, the rescuer, why the corporations send repair robots.
- **Comedy and tone:** eerie / meta moments in the world.
- **Ways to make the endless city feel alive and surprising**, rather than repeating.
