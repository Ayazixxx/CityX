# What other players see: movement, items, hair, damage

Everything below is owner-authoritative (the player's own machine decides, the server relays) and is tested over real sockets by `NetworkedPlayerStateSyncPlayTests`
(a server and two clients). The standing rule: a new thing a player can do or have is not finished until other players can see it (abilities: `docs/ABILITIES_MULTIPLAYER.md`).

## Joining and leaving (2026-11-05)

Found by reading the code rather than in play, and tested in-process: **nobody has yet tried these on two real machines.**

- **A friend's body waits for their world.** A friend connects from the title screen and only then picks a character and loads the city. Spawned on connecting, the body landed in the title scene and was destroyed with it, unknown to the server, leaving them in the city with no body and no controls, unless they reached the world before the host did. Now their copy of `NetworkedPlayerSpawner` broadcasts `WorldLoadedMessage` once a second until they have a body (the host may not be in the world yet), and the server spawns it on the first. A connection with no body also fails every view-distance check, so the host's body waits too.
- **The HUD and menus follow the body played.** They are wired at build time to the baked player, which on a friend's machine is the host's body, so bars showed the wrong numbers and no death screen came up. `LocalPlayerBody.Claim` (from `NetworkedPlayerIdentity`, for a spawned clone) has `LocalBodyBinder` repoint them. Since 2026-11-06 (bug hunt N5) this includes what the friend does with their own hands: ReDraw repaints their body (`PlayerRedrawStarter.Body`), hold-E carry works from their body (the networked player has its own `GrabCarry`, looking from this machine's camera), and the loot window, inspect card and prompt read their body (`LocalPlayerBody.Find`, never the first body with a reader, which could be the host's copy). A friend joining with a new character gets the intro fall on their own body once it is claimed (`IntroCutsceneStarter.PlayOnClaimedBody`, bug hunt N2), not on the host's, whose input it used to switch back on for the friend's keys.
- **Only the machine playing a body takes its keys.** The input reader is off from `Awake` in a session and on only for the owner. It used to be turned off in `OnStartClient`, which never runs for a body beyond the host's view radius, so the host's keys walked a far friend's body.
- **Leaving ends the session** (`NetworkBootstrap.LeaveSession`): Quit To Title stops the connection and destroys the manager. A client whose connection stops (the host quit, the line dropped) goes back to the title too.
- **A session's clock is nobody's to stop** (2026-11-13, docs/MULTIPLAYER_MOVEMENT.md): Escape used to stop the clock in a session too, freezing the pausing player on every screen and every other body on theirs; there the pause menu now only takes the keys (`NetworkBootstrap.PlaysAlone`). And a session runs on when its window is not in front (it used to stop, and the player was dropped).

## Adding a new held item

Implement `ICosmeticMountable` on its component (copy `SwordWeapon`), and add its prefab to `NetworkedPlayerWiring.ItemPrefabPaths`.
`EquipmentReplicationGuardTests` fails otherwise.

Its pickup in the world notices only THIS machine's player: give it a `GroundPickup` and hand its `OnTriggerEnter` and `OnTriggerExit`
to it, as every pickup does (inside, `InteractionReach.LocalPlayerBody` and `IsLeaving`). Until 2026-10-17 each took whichever body
walked in last, so an NPC or another player's copy passing through left E doing nothing, and hats and clothes forgot the player when an
arm swung out (`PickupReachPlayTests`).

## Adding a new piece of shared world state

Something of the world that every machine must agree on (as the light switches, the balcony doors, the glass, the graffiti, the dropped
items, the weather and the enemies do) derives from `SessionRelay`: it finds the session, tells the relay when to hook and unhook its
broadcasts as this machine becomes the server or a client (`HearAsServer`, `HearAsClient`, with `Hear<T>` for each message), and when a
joining player may ask for what it missed (`OnLetIn`). The server checks every ask (`WireChecks`, `SenderRate`).
