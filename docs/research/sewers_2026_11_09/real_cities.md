# Real layered cities: flood tunnels, sewers, vertical drops (research notes, 2026-11-09)

Method and honesty note. These come from web searches and page fetches in one session. The search tool and page reader were weak: many pages came back as summaries, several PDFs were unreadable, and a few sites refused. Every line carries a source URL. Lines tagged **[UNVERIFIED]** or **[GENERAL KNOWLEDGE]** are not backed by an opened source. Sizes are real-world metres; multiply by about 1.3 for the 2.6 m player. Las Vegas channels, manholes, box culverts and trash racks are left out on purpose.

## 1. Chongqing

**What I could actually find was thin.** Chinese engineering pages on named box culverts (桃花溪, 清水溪, 盘溪河) gave no cross-sections. Treat everything below as context, not as dimensions.

- **The old peninsula's streams were buried and became sewers.** On the Yuzhong peninsula the streams Shuangxigou, Daxigou and Huangshaxi became sewers or vanished into housing; Haitangxi (south bank) was entirely covered 20-30 years ago. Sources: https://dfz.cq.gov.cn/zqlswh/rwby_417819/202312/t20231220_12727655.html (reported via search summary; the page itself did not open in a direct fetch), https://www.turenscape.com/news/detail/724.html (opened).
- **Buried streams are far too small for floods.** An interviewee in the Turenscape piece says the original channels were tens of metres wide and the replacement sewers only a few metres wide; some streams had pipes laid in advance that were too narrow to carry floods. Haitangxi's middle reach was filled in from the 1980s; a stone bridge from the backwater pond to the Yangtze was demolished in 2001 for the Nanbin road; the pond was filled in 2003. Source: https://www.turenscape.com/news/detail/724.html (opened). Game use: a surviving stream is a tunnel much wider than its street.
- **Qing-dynasty drainage ditches follow the slope.** Under Emperor Qianlong the old city had 17 drainage ditches, 11 into the Yangtze and 6 into the Jialing; some used city gates as their outlets, and the names Dayanggou and Jianjiaqiao survive. Remnants (Qujiagou, Huishuigou) near Shibati stair street still carry household sewage. Source: search summary of http://district.ce.cn/newarea/roll/201308/23/t20130823_24685362.shtml (that page failed to open: certificate error; the claim is NOT confirmed first-hand) and the Yuzhong item above.
- **Rule for building drainage on hills.** A Chongqing design code exists: "Code for Design of Outdoor Drainage Pipeline in Mountainous City" (Chongqing Municipal Design and Research Institute, 2018). Contents not retrieved. Source: cited in https://mdpi.com/2073-4441/13/16/2277 (search summary; not opened).
- **Terrain drives failure.** A 2024 Ecological Indicators paper on Chongqing's main urban basin says terrain is the main driver of cascading drainage failure; low valley areas collect overflow fast. Source: https://doaj.org/article/f89dad798d914e839b8772776b6d1eda (search summary; not opened).
- **Drop manholes are the standard answer on steep catchments** (general hydraulic literature): they exist to dissipate energy so downstream velocity stays safe. Source: https://bvbvf1.bib-bvb.de/vufind/PrimoRecord/cdi_proquest_journals_2565721033/Details (search summary).
- **Air-raid tunnels (防空洞, locally 洞子).** More than 1,600 shelters, about 1.1 million m2 of usable floor; in 1943 about 1,500 private shelters totalling 65,000 m of passage held about 330,000 people. Passages are narrow with long steep steps and many corners; year-round temperature above 20 C. Reuse since 2018 under the "山城洞天" programme, one plan per shelter: cooling spots, hotpot restaurants, bookstores, a petrol station, wine cellars (Liziba, Yuzhong). Source: https://www.ourchinastory.com/cn/amp/9247 (opened). A reused tunnel gets hardened floor plus power, water and drainage lines (Liangjiang district markets). Source: https://epaper.cqrb.cn/cqrb/2025-08/13/005/cqrb20250813005.pdf and https://m.gmw.cn/2026-08/02/content_1304541840.htm (search summaries only).
- **A shelter reused as a walkway.** "洞见山城": 320 m long with a 30 m drop in height, linking Hongyadong, Daijiaxiang and the Jialing riverside road. Source: search summary of the Chongqing Daily e-paper pages above; not opened. **[UNVERIFIED detail]**
- **Layered building, entered from two ground levels.** Hongyadong is on a cliff above the Jialing; reported as 11 floors, floor 11 meets Cangbai Road (towards Jiefangbei), floor 1 meets the Jiabin riverside road, 4 horizontal streets and several vertical stairways between; floors 1 and 11 are also joined by internal lifts and stairs. Source: search summary of https://touch.travel.qunar.com/poi/709121 and https://www.eastchinatrip.com/zh-hant/hongya-cave-taiwan-travel-guide/ (tourist sites, not opened; floor-by-floor accounts disagree).
- **Riverside lower levels do flood.** 19 Aug 2020: the Jialing flooded outside Hongyadong at 9:50, the whole first floor of the Hongyadong scenic area went under, the Chaotianmen gate was submerged to the three characters by 14:00. Gauge forecasts: Caiyuanba 7.5-8.5 m above the guaranteed level, Cuntan 7-7.5 m, Ciqikou 8-9 m. Ciqikou reading 191.37 m, Cuntan 191.55 m, 0.14 m above the 1981 record. Sources: https://www.ichongqing.info/2020/08/19/chongqing-launches-class-%e2%85%a0-flood-emergency-response-lower-streets-of-downtown-under-water/amp (opened), https://english.liangjiang.gov.cn/2020-08/21/c_854196_2.htm and https://www.taiwannews.com.tw/en/news/3975669 (search summaries). So the water rise is on the order of 8-9 m: whole street levels go under.
- **Sponge-city.** Chongqing reports 466 drainage partitions up to standard (40% of built-up area), with different routes for mountain, deep-canyon, hill and flat-dam terrain; visible structures are rain gardens, permeable paving, and detention tanks (调蓄池). No dimensions. Source: https://paper.people.com.cn/rmrb/html/2024-04/26/nw.D110000renmrb_20240426_1-07.htm (opened).
- **NOT found:** outfalls in retaining walls (堡坎), stepped drainage channels beside stair streets (梯坎), named culvert cross-sections, any deep interceptor tunnel for Chongqing. Searches returned nothing relevant (the one "deep tunnel" hit was the Yuxia high-speed rail Yangtze tunnel: shield 12.66 m, https://www.stdaily.com/web/gdxw/2026-08/21/content_567780.html, search summary; not a drainage item). Weir/step energy dissipation in Chinese gutters is only known from a hydraulics paper, not from Chongqing walls.

## 2. Other steep or layered cities

### Hong Kong
- **HK West Drainage Tunnel (HKWDT).** About 10.5-11 km main tunnel from Tai Hang to near Cyberport, internal diameter 6.25 to 7.25 m (some sources say up to 7.3 m); about 8 km of adits; 34 intake structures; 2 portals. Commissioned 22 Aug 2012. Designed to discharge up to 240 m3/s at the final outlet portal. Sources: https://www.arup.com/projects/hong-kong-west-drainage-tunnel/ (opened: 10.5 km, 32 dropshafts, 240 m3/s), https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hong_Kong_West_Drainage_Tunnel (opened), https://hkie.org.hk/ea/en/project/153 (opened: up to 7.3 m, 10.7 km, 34 intakes), search summary of https://www.iahr.org/library/info?pid=2692 (6.25-7.25 m, 34 intakes).
- **Dropshafts.** 32 dropshafts, up to 195 m deep per Arup (another presentation says 175 m); most built by raise boring. Source: https://www.arup.com/projects/hong-kong-west-drainage-tunnel/ (opened).
- **Intake structure parts.** Bottom rack, bottom rack chamber, link channel to a spiral vortex inlet, then the dropshaft; a fully vertical shaft was hard to build so CFD tested an inclined shaft at 1 in 200. Source: search summary of the IAHR/Arup items above; not opened. Shaft diameter: **not found**.
- **Where the intakes are.** The tunnel intercepts runoff from the Mid-Levels mountainside above the dense urban area and sends it straight to sea. Sources: https://www.arup.com/projects/hong-kong-west-drainage-tunnel/ (opened).
- **Other tunnels.** Lai Chi Kok about 3.7 km; Tsuen Wan about 5.1 km, along Tuen Mun Highway to Rambler Channel. Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hong_Kong_West_Drainage_Tunnel (opened).
- **Catchwater channels.** About 93 km of hillside catchwater channels and 9 km of catchwater tunnels, some without vehicle access. Source: search summary of https://www.devb.gov.hk/filemanager/en/content_420/article.pdf (not opened).
- **Underground tanks under sports pitches.** Tai Hang Tung (Mong Kok): 100,000 m3, completed 2004, pumps 3 x 0.95 m3/s emptying in 15 h (dsd.gov.hk/EN/HTML/395.html and https://hkie.org.hk/hkietransactions/doc.php?id=960, search summaries). A single secondary source gives 136 x 130 x 7.5 m beneath rugby and football pitches (https://ice.org.uk/what-is-civil-engineering/infrastructure-projects/trio-storm-water-storage-hong-kong), **not confirmed by DSD**. Happy Valley: 60,000 m3, L-shaped under five sports pitches, pump house 1.5 m3/s, commissioned March 2017 (https://iwa-network.org/hong-kong-happy-valley-underground-stormwater-storage-scheme/ and https://www.dsd.gov.hk/others/HVUSSS/doc/newsletter/project_booklet_2018.pdf, search summaries). Game use: a flat roof is a football pitch.
- **Decked nullah.** Tai Hang: a 250 m twin-cell box culvert; the deck widened the footpath and became the "Fire Dragon Path". Cell sizes not given. Source: https://www.dsd.gov.hk/EN/What_s_New/What_s_New/EventOther133.html (opened). Sixteen nullah sections were to be decked over a decade from 2005 (https://www.news.gov.hk/isd/ebulletin/en/category/environment/050114/print/050114en04007.htm, search summary).

### Tokyo
- **G-Cans (Metropolitan Area Outer Underground Discharge Channel).** Five silos, each 65 m tall and 32 m in diameter; 6.4 km of tunnel linking them, 50 m below the surface; a water tank 25.4 m high, 177 m long, 78 m wide with 59 pillars; 78 pumps of 10 MW. Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Metropolitan_Area_Outer_Underground_Discharge_Channel (opened). Tunnel inner diameter was not in the page. **[UNVERIFIED: I recall about 10 m, general knowledge]**
- **Kanda River / Loop Road No. 7 tunnel.** Inner diameter 12.5 m, outer 13.7 m, about 4.5 km, 34-43 m under Loop 7, stores 540,000 m3; first 2 km opened 1997; start shaft 60.1 m deep and 28.2 m in diameter; 505,000 m3 stored in Typhoon Melor (2009). Source: search summary of https://www.jsce.or.jp/kokusai/civil_engineering/1998/urbanunder.pdf and Tokyo government pages (not opened individually). Tunnels run deeper than Tokyo's subway lines, about 42 m down, a one-minute lift ride. Source: https://www.unitracc.com/aktuelles/artikel/tokyo-averts-floods-with-an-intricate-cavern-of-underground-tunnels-and-vaults (opened). Tokyo has 28 regulating reservoirs: 16 in-ground, 9 underground vaults, 3 tunnels (same page).

### Others
- **Genoa (covered torrents).** The Fereggiano diversion: a 42 m deep intake shaft into an expansion chamber, then a 3.7 km tunnel about 5.2 m across to the sea (one source says 6 m; unresolved). The historic Bisagno culvert is 50 m wide at its downstream end. The Bisagno diversion is about 6.65 km long, with a side spillway intake and sluice gates. Sources: https://tunnelsandtunnelling.com/?p=8386, https://cmcgruppo.com/en/progetti/bisagno-stream-flood-diversion-tunnel-in-the-municipality-of-genoa/ and https://iris.unige.it/bitstream/11567/1146199/1/1173%20_%20120%20_%20Culverted%20rivers%20in%20the%20historic%20center%20of%20Genoa%20%28Italy%29%20as%20an%20emblematic%20case%20of%20human%20pressure%20and%20fluvial%20landscape%20changes%20_%20F.%20Faccini1%2c%20P.%20Piana1%2c%20S.%20Saj2%2c%20M.%20Terrone3%20%e2%80%93%20Eugeo%209th%20Congress.pdf (all search summaries; not opened).
- **Kuala Lumpur SMART tunnel.** 9.7 km stormwater tunnel, outer diameter 13.2 m, with a double-deck 4 km motorway inside for light vehicles; modes 1-4: floodwater first goes through a bypass under the road, then the road closes, then gates open and water uses the road decks; reopens within 48 h. Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/SMART_Tunnel (opened).
- **Seoul.** Dorimcheon deep tunnel planned about 4.54 km long, 10.4-12.0 m in diameter, 40-50 m underground; an existing Sinwol tunnel is reported as 4.7 km and 10 m. Source: https://sbsstar.net/article/N1008622600/45-km-deep-underground-rainwater-tunnel-under-dorimcheon-stream-seouls-flood-defense-system-begins-in-earnest and https://www.asiae.co.kr/en/article/2024111010115379948 (search summaries, dates disagree).
- **Istanbul Basilica Cistern.** 336 columns in 12 rows of 28, each about 9 m tall, spaced 4.8-4.9 m; hall about 138-143 m by 64-70 m. Source: https://www.basilica-cistern.com/facts and https://marmaraeah.saglik.gov.tr/EN-1159979/basilica-cistern.html (search summaries, tourist figures vary). A 40.75 m column height on one page is an error.
- **London Tideway.** 7.2 m diameter ("three double-deckers"), 31 m deep at Acton falling to 66 m at Abbey Mills (1 m per 790 m); 21 shafts, most channel overflow flows into the tunnel. Source: https://tideway.london/the-tunnel/ (opened). Chambers Wharf shaft walls 1.5 m thick, just over 72 m deep (search summary, https://tunneltalk.com/UK-26Sep19-Four-TBMs-underway-on-Tideway-with-fifth-ready-to-launch.php).
- **Naples:** not researched.

## 3. How water goes down a level

- **Vortex drop shaft.** Water enters through a circular, scroll, spiral or tangential inlet, spins down the wall, a central air core stays open; most energy goes into wall friction. Deaeration chamber at the base is common when discharging into closed conduits. Used widely to connect shallow and deep sewers. Sources: https://image-ppubs.uspto.gov/dirsearch-public/print/downloadPdf/4790355 and https://www.iahr.org/library/info?pid=23967 (search summaries).
- **Plunge shaft.** Water falls free into a "plunge pool" or energy dissipator at the bottom; the base structures are a major share of cost. Baffle drops cascade shelf to shelf, plunging on each. Source: Chicago TARP search summary: https://vtchl.illinois.edu/tunnel-and-reservoir-plan/ and https://static.elitesecurity.org/uploads/4/1/4106216/BaffleDropStructureDesign.pdf (not opened).
- **Real sizes.** Chicago TARP: over 250 drop shafts, diameters 4 to 17 feet (1.2-5.2 m) on the Mainstream system, up to 25 feet (7.6 m) elsewhere; tunnels 3-10 m across, 150-360 ft (46-110 m) down. Source: https://onemine.org/documents/shafts-and-tunnels-damen-avenue-to-roosevelt-road (search summary). Genoa Fereggiano: 42 m shaft (above). Hong Kong: up to 195 m (above).
- **Stepped spillway (tumbling bays).** Three regimes: nappe flow (water falls step to step with splash) at small flows, skimming flow (a coherent stream over the step edges, eddies in the cavities) at large flows; transition flow splashes violently. Step heights not given by the source. Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stepped_spillway (opened). Sizes **[GENERAL KNOWLEDGE]**: steps of roughly 0.3-1 m on dam chutes.
- **Drop manholes** exist to dissipate energy on steep catchments (Chongqing section).

## 4. Old brick sewers

- **Paris classes (cleanly sourced).** Elementary sewer under each street: ovoid, 1.30 m wide. Secondary collector 3 m wide with a 1.20 m cunette. Principal collector (under boulevards) 5-6 m wide with a 3.50 m cunette. Outfall (émissaire, not walkable) round, 2.5-6 m. Ivry-Masséna tunnel 1.86 km, 6.8 m diameter, about 30 m deep, 80,000 m3. Source: https://fr.wikipedia.org/wiki/Égouts_de_Paris (opened; the visible part of the text only).
- **Banquette height and width:** NOT found. The museum page says broad paved walkways run both sides of the main tunnels; pipes (water, telecom, pneumatic tubes) run along the ceiling; headroom lets most people walk upright; temperature 13 C. Source: https://showcaves.com/english/fr/subterranea/ParisEgouts.html (opened). The museum tour is 500 m of real sewer (same page).
- **Street plaques: verified.** Street-name plates sit at the corner of every gallery, as on the surface; building numbers appear in places; wide tunnels lie under wide boulevards, small ones under small streets. Sources: https://www.pariszigzag.fr/insolite/lieux-insolites/anecdotes-egouts-paris/ (opened) and https://www.paris.fr/pages/les-egouts-a-paris-2367/ (opened).
- **Slope.** Belgrand used 3 cm per metre so a person could walk safely and sand moved. Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paris_sewers (opened).
- **Flushing.** Bateau-vanne: about 10 m long, 2.5 m wide, over 9 tonnes (the French Wikipedia says nearly 5 tonnes: sources disagree), a mobile dam on the sewer floor that holds water behind a gate and then opens it for a 1-3 m3/s surge; it stops at a desilting basin where a truck sucks out the sand; used every two years in main collectors, with wagons-vannes in narrower ones. Source: https://www.paris.fr/pages/le-bateau-vanne-a-retrouve-les-egouts-36599 (opened). Flushing tanks (réservoirs de chasse) at the head of each elementary sewer; about 4,000 built by 1900; storm overflow galleries (déversoirs d'orage) to the Seine; flood pumping stations along the river; about 6,000 m3 of sand removed a year. Sources: https://www.paris.fr/pages/les-egouts-a-paris-2367/ and https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paris_sewers (both opened).
- **Cleaning ball (boule de curage).** Wood and iron balls of varying sizes rolled through blockages; the water pushes it and solids are always pushed ahead of it. Diameter **not found**. Source: https://www.atlasobscura.com/articles/3117 (403 on fetch; summary from a search result only).
- **Vienna.** Egg profile with the point at the bottom for speed at low flow; standard sizes (height x width, cm): NP I 70x105, NP II 80x120, NP III 90x135, NP IV 100x150, NP VI 120x180 (that is 0.7-1.2 m high). Access shafts about every 80 m; separate cleaning shafts on collectors; inverted siphons under the Danube and Donaukanal; grit traps; about 2,400 km, naturally ventilated. Source: https://www.wien.gv.at/umwelt/kanal/lexikon/ (opened). The Third Man tour runs at about 7 m depth (https://www.falter.at/zeitung/20230808/sieben-meter-unter-der-erde, search summary). The tour's own corridor widths: **not found**.
- **Brighton.** Interior dimensions not found; the network ranges from 30 cm pipes to 2.4 m circular brick tunnels (search summary, https://www.subbrit.org.uk/sites/brighton-sewers/); tour entry through pier arch 260 under the Palace Pier, with steps and ladders; a 1990s storm drain "wide enough to drive a vehicle through" reaches tunnel depth by capped deep shafts under the beach; one emergency storm outfall is visible in the stone groyne next to the Palace Pier. Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brighton_sewers (opened). Some sources say the public tours stopped in 2019.
- **London Bazalgette, Hamburg, Prague:** not researched with sources. **[GENERAL KNOWLEDGE]:** Bazalgette's Victorian intercepting sewers are brick, egg-shaped or circular and drain by gravity to pumping stations such as Abbey Mills (cruciform engine hall, eight beam engines; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Abbey_Mills_Pumping_Station, opened, with no sewer dimensions).
- **Penstocks, junction chamber sizes, ladder spacing:** not found. Vienna lists the shaft spacing only.

## 5. Where trains and drainage meet

Channel width and depth not given. Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Los_Angeles_River (opened; first part only).
- **Depth bands (what I can source).** Tideway sewer 31-66 m. Kanda 34-43 m, deeper than Tokyo subway lines. Seoul planned 40-50 m. Chicago 46-110 m. G-Cans 50 m. Paris Ivry-Masséna about 30 m. Vienna Third Man corridor about 7 m. Hong Kong dropshafts up to 195 m. Sources as cited above. A clean "subway X m, sewer Y m" table with examples **was not found**; **[GENERAL KNOWLEDGE]**: old brick sewers sit a few metres under the street, subways at roughly 10-30 m, deep storm tunnels deeper than both.

## 6. Entrances a person can use

- Outfall at the beach: Brighton storm outfall visible in the groyne next to the pier (Brighton item above).
- Hillside adits: HKWDT's roughly 8 km of adits are the real-world version of a side tunnel driven in from a slope (https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hong_Kong_West_Drainage_Tunnel, opened). Where they open on the surface I could not confirm.
- Shaft buildings and lifts: Kanda/Loop 7 is about 42 m down and reached by lift in about a minute (unitracc page, opened); the tunnel start shaft is 60.1 m deep and 28.2 m wide (search summary).
- Reused air-raid tunnels with two hillside mouths: Chongqing's walkway-shelter links one road to another 30 m lower (section 1; unverified detail).
- Street-level access shafts: Vienna every ~80 m (lexikon page, opened).
- Culvert mouths under bridges, retaining-wall outfalls, cliff outfalls: no sourced examples found for hill cities.

## Ideas for a layered game city

1. **Buried streams under streets, much wider than the street above.** From the Chongqing streams that were tens of metres wide and replaced by narrow sewers: make "stream tunnels" that are 20-40 m wide old valleys with a thin modern pipe in one corner.
2. **Hundred-metre-class shafts as the big set piece.** From HK dropshafts up to 195 m and Genoa's 42 m intake: one huge vertical shaft with a stairwell or ledge path around its wall.
3. **Vortex shaft you can look down.** From the spiral vortex inlet with an open air core: a water spiral round a dark hole, with a ledge walkway over the link channel before it.
4. **Hillside intake structures at the top.** From HKWDT intakes catching Mid-Levels runoff: a rack and chamber set into a retaining wall at the top of a slope, the entry point from the upper street.
5. **A football pitch roof.** From Tai Hang Tung and Happy Valley tanks under sports pitches: a huge low flat-ceilinged tank under a visible pitch, pump house at one side.
6. **Pillared hall.** From the G-Cans tank (59 pillars, 25.4 m high, 177 x 78 m) and the Basilica Cistern (336 columns, 9 m): a rhythmic grid of columns, ankle-deep water, wading to waist depth at the low end.
7. **Silos.** From G-Cans silos 65 m x 32 m: round shafts with lift or stair, used as the connection between city levels.
8. **Street plaques underground.** From Paris street-name plates at each gallery corner: put the street name of the road above on tunnel corners; big tunnels under big streets.
9. **Three Paris classes.** From the 1.3 m / 3 m / 5-6 m galleries with 1.2 m and 3.5 m cunettes: use narrow crawl galleries, medium walkable ones, and wide collectors with banquettes and a central channel.
10. **Road inside a flood tunnel.** From the SMART tunnel (a road deck inside a 13.2 m tunnel, closed in modes 3-4): a tunnel that is a road at ground level and floods at one level, with gates and warning signs.
11. **Buried cliff entrance.** From Hongyadong (11 floors, entered from both the top street and the river road, whole bottom floor flooded in 2020): a building whose lower storeys are the flood floor with water marks on the walls up to its 2nd floor.
12. **Flushing boat as a set piece.** From the bateau-vanne (10 m x 2.5 m, gate-dam, 1-3 m3/s surge): a stranded flush-boat in a collector, and a timed surge that sweeps the central channel.
