# Sewers and flood tunnels: game craft research (2026-11-09)

Web research only. Source quality is mixed: web search returned summaries, and several developer pages could not be read in full. Every claim has a URL. "OWN" marks my general knowledge or inference, not from a source. A list of what I could not verify is at the end of section 6's neighbour, "Could not verify".

All numbers in metres unless a source states game units.

## 1. Third-person cameras in tight spaces

**Found**
- Level Design Book metrics table: minimum hallway width is at least double the player width. Unity reference: player 1.0 x 1.8 m, hallway 2.0 m, door 1.25 x 2.5 m, wall height 3.0 m. Unreal reference: hallway 1.5 m. Doors should be narrower than hallways. Source: https://book.leveldesignbook.com/process/blockout/metrics
- Bethesda kit talk (Burgess/Purkeypile, GDC 2013, transcript): character about 128 units (six feet); minimum traversable width at least two character widths so two AI can pass; one uniform door-frame size across all kits. Source: https://gamedeveloper.com/design/skyrim-s-modular-approach-to-level-design
- Stray's camera write-up (Unwound devlog): spherecast from the player toward the intended camera spot; on a hit the camera lerps in, but jumps if an obstacle is right in front (so it never clips). The camera stays at the blocked distance until there is space, then "dollies back"; Stray's delay before dolly-back is roughly 2 to 3 seconds; slower dolly-back suits big spaces and faster suits small ones. "This is almost always mitigated by level design." Looking up while pressed to a wall tilted the camera badly, so look-up was made intent-only. Near the player the camera rises above them and the player fades, with first-person swap as an option. Source: https://joshhirshfield.substack.com/p/development-dive-unwounds-custom
- Free-move-zone camera article (Game Developer): the camera collider is a sphere that slides along geometry and this rule beats all others. When the character is occluded it tries, in order: rotate around, move closer keeping a minimum distance, move closer plus rotate, and last resort move closer until inside the character mesh. No numbers given. Source: https://gamedeveloper.com/design/third-person-camera-design-with-free-move-zone
- Search summary of Epic's "Six ingredients" blog (page itself returned 403, not read): usual pattern is to bring the camera closer when a wall obstructs; near-plane corners can clip walls even when a centre ray is clear; Epic points to John Nesky's GDC talk "50 Camera Mistakes" (Journey) as essential. Source: https://www.unrealengine.com/tech-blog/six-ingredients-for-a-dynamic-third-person-camera
- GamesRadar piece "Why are the doors so big in video games" says Gears of War doors are huge for the camera to pass (only the search summary was readable; article body failed). Source: https://www.gamesradar.com/why-are-the-doors-so-big-in-video-games-some-developers-explain

**Not found:** no published ratio of corridor width to character height for shipped third-person games, no ceiling-height-to-character ratios, and no named talks on Uncharted/Gears/Last of Us camera narrowing.

**OWN inference for this game** (character 2.6 m, camera 5 m back, head top at mid screen, so the camera sits roughly head height or above, about 2.5 to 3 m): a corridor camera needs ceiling clearance of more than 3.5 m, or it will pull in constantly. Practical rule: main channels 5 m wide or more and 4.5 m or higher; "crawl" side passages are allowed but expect the camera to collapse close behind the player, so test them with the aim camera (2.9 m back) as the pull-in target. Side-step bends need radius, since a sphere cast sweeping round a tight inside corner jitters (OWN, common experience).

## 2. Making darkness playable

**Found**
- Metro Exodus: with ray tracing the world is lit only where light reaches, so the flashlight becomes the main tool; with RT off the game is "largely" playable without it. Original had artificially dark pre-baked lighting. Sources: https://blogs.nvidia.com/?p=41915 (via search summary), https://www.pcgamer.com/metro-exodus-enhanced-edition-personal-pick-game-of-the-year. Press, not developer talks.
- Dying Light: The Beast director Nathan Lemaire: "Night is truly pitch black", flashlight lights only a few metres, flashlight attracts the Volatile enemies; nights vary by biome (forest total dark, towns get moon and ambient). Dying Light 2's team admitted they "overdid it" and tuned to a middle ground. Sources: https://gamingbolt.com/dying-light-the-beast-reinvents-the-night-heres-why-that-matters, https://www.playstationlifestyle.net/?p=886481 (search summaries; not opened).
- Darkwood (Game Developer): visibility mechanics hide detail; lighting "intended to conceal things at least as much as it was to illuminate them"; static scenery stays visible outside the cone but items, enemies and hazards only inside it; motion seen without knowing its cause is effective. Sources: https://gamedeveloper.com/design/how-i-darkwood-i-s-visibility-mechanics-create-a-new-kind-of-horror, https://store.epicgames.com/news/darkwood-is-a-horror-masterpiece-viewed-from-an-atypical-perspective
- Amnesia (Thomas Grip): "Good horror often comes from claustrophobic spaces and thrives on low visibility"; the first design used darkness as the enemy and was dropped; light sources are resources, and he regretted scattering too many; small differences in monitor brightness changed how players experienced the system and made balance hard. Source: https://www.gamedeveloper.com/design/game-design-deep-dive-i-amnesia-i-s-sanity-meter-
- Alien: Isolation 2: Creative Assembly built custom lighting because engine defaults are tuned for brighter scenes; flashlight is both a light and a beacon to the enemy in the original. Sources: https://www.techradar.com/gaming/creative-assembly-says-alien-isolation-2s-custom-tech-enabled-a-realistic-atmosphere-unreal-engine-5-couldnt-we-felt-that-a-custom-solution-would-give-us-that-perfect-frame-response-that-you-want-in-that-kind-of-really-really-dark-environment, https://blog.playstation.com/2014/03/26/first-look-behind-the-terror-of-alien-isolation/. No fill-light numbers found.
- Gamma advantage: Escape from Tarkov's studio threatened bans for brightening tools; Hunt: Showdown players still report darkness problems with gamma maxed; Phasmophobia developer said a minimum brightness was "being worked on". No developer-published solution found. Sources: https://www.gamepressure.com/S013-amp.asp?ID=20685, https://steamcommunity.com/app/594650/discussions/8/3190241719885601086, https://reddit.birdcat.cafe/r/PhasmophobiaGame/comments/15xjpai/lighting_update_why_when_and_where/jx6rcwz/?context=3
- Flashlight cones: engines use an inner cone at full brightness and an outer cone that fades; default spot angle in the Unity-style manual is 30 degrees; a forum comment suggests "bigger cone, less brightness" reads better; a light cookie texture is the other option. Sources: https://docs.unrealengine.com/4.27/BuildingWorlds/LightingAndShadows/LightTypes/Spot, https://forums.unrealengine.com/t/horror-game-flashlight-problem/69671

**Not found:** exact fill-light levels, wet-speculars guidance, or any toon/cel-specific dark-scene guidance. OWN knowledge, no source: (a) keep a very low constant ambient (a few percent) plus an unlit or emissive rim on characters so silhouettes always read, and since the game has black outlines, outlines themselves carry silhouette in the dark; (b) flashlight wide (outer 50 to 70 degrees, inner about 25) with a second weak, wide point light near the player as fake bounce; (c) to stop gamma abuse in multiplayer, enforce a floor server-side: do not rely on the lit scene alone, apply a fixed post-process black-level lift and a clamped exposure that ignores the player's brightness slider below a minimum, and tell players the slider is calibrated by a test image (Amnesia notes the problem but gives no fix).

## 3. Mazes that stay fun

**Found**
- Sewer criticism (forums and listicles, so opinion): clichéd; "uniformity of twisting corridors acts as an excuse to create a generic maze"; straight corridors, no tactical freedom; ugly and drab; feels like a chore after a good area; the same encounter repeated for 15 minutes; water slows controls. Praise goes to variety of approaches (Metro sewers), distinct identity (bright, fast sewer stage), and strong narrative (FF7 Remake). Sources: https://forums.escapistmagazine.com/threads/what-makes-sewer-levels-so-bad.276984/, https://www.tentonhammer.com/columns/the-reason-why-no-game-should-have-a-sewer-level, https://sidequest.zone/2025/03/03/video-game-sewers/ (search summaries).
- Left 4 Dead 2: Valve's Alex Vlachos (SIGGRAPH 2010 Advances in Real-Time Rendering) says early playtests found players confused in the swamps, so flowing water was used to point towards the route; flow maps cost 2 extra texture fetches (flow and noise). The PDF is binary and could not be read, I rely on the search summary. Source: https://www.advances.realtimerendering.com/s2010/Vlachos-Waterflow(SIGGRAPH%202010%20Advanced%20RealTime%20Rendering%20Course).pdf. A level-design article repeats "a subtle flow of the water guiding the player" and notes the AI Director opens one route and closes others, and that narrow halls got dangerous once Chargers and Spitters were added. Source: https://worldofleveldesign.com/categories/left4dead_mapping/l4d2-level-design-tips-part2.php
- Level Design Book: horror layouts can use sharp narrow turns, blind intersections, S-curves and maze-like dead ends; a dead-end whose exit is visible on the way back encourages exploring it; "loopback" structure is common in open-world dungeons; vistas preview landmarks. Landmarks (stand-out architecture) are reference points; levels without them cause navigation problems. A secondary guide suggests wrong paths be darker and dead-end quickly. Sources: https://https-book_leveldesignbook_com.proxy.bugsmash.io/process/layout/typology and /process/layout/flow (proxy mirrors of book.leveldesignbook.com), https://unity.com/blog/level-design-basics
- Elden Ring: the Subterranean Shunning-Grounds is disliked (called "Elden Ring's Blighttown": layout, enemy placement), while a reviewer says Leyndell's sewers lean on the combat-room trap. Source: https://www.gamesradar.com/im-300-hours-into-elden-ring-and-have-only-now-discovered-its-version-of-dark-souls-worst-area, https://www.backloggd.com/u/faea/review/1238271
- Warframe's tile tree: one reachable path start to exit, no overlaps; players say it should be more like a web than a tree. The Division Underground reviews: random dungeons repeat the same areas. Sources: https://wiki.warframe.com/w/Tileset, https://forums.warframe.com/topic/1328858-tileset-layout, https://gamingbolt.com/the-division-underground-dlc-review

**Not found:** a measured "how long a featureless stretch can run" figure anywhere. OWN rule of thumb, unsourced: a plain stretch past about 30 to 40 seconds of walking (about 150 m at run speed) needs a landmark, a camp light, a junction or a set-piece.

## 4. Procedural undergrounds

**Found**
- Bethesda kit (Skyrim, Fallout): footprint is a 3D bounding box all pieces fit; sizes are multiples (512 room with 256 hallway tiles cleanly, 384 does not); hallways tile on one axis, rooms on two; designers snap at half the footprint; do not build to or past the edge; alcoves must stay inside the footprint; stacking vertically uses a separate fixed-horizontal-size sub-kit with predetermined floor thickness to avoid co-planar ceilings and floors; avoid "patch-up" pieces; stress-test by looping the kit back on itself; pivots at ground-plane centre; "01" suffix for visual variants. Source: https://gamedeveloper.com/design/skyrim-s-modular-approach-to-level-design
- Deep Rock Galactic: seed is created when the mission is picked; cave built from 100+ hand-made templates (119 cited), each made of spheres, half-spheres and planes defining carved volume; orange half-spheres mark exits that become graph nodes; rooms linked "like pearls on a string" by narrow winding tunnels; templates warped, mirrored, overlapped; biomes add noise and debris. The article does not say how reachability is guaranteed. A Reddit comment claims marching tetrahedra, unverified. Source: https://devtrackers.gg/deep-rock-galactic/p/8cd5ca9c-below-decks-at-ghost-ship-cave-generation-in-deep-rock-galactic
- Unexplored (Dormans): graph first. A mission graph with a large loop on a 5x5 grid is built by rewrite rules (about 40 rules for the loop), 24 cycle types give pacing, minor cycles and dead ends are added up to a size target, abstract nodes resolve later; the grid is doubled for corridors and expanded 5x to tiles; nodes never move so overlap is avoided by design. About 5,000 rules. Sources: https://www.boristhebrave.com/2021/04/10/dungeon-generation-in-unexplored/, https://gamedeveloper.com/design/unexplored-s-secret-cyclic-dungeon-generation-
- Warframe tiles: tree of tiles with no overlap and one path (wiki). Source: https://wiki.warframe.com/w/Tileset
- Division Underground: random mazes of subway, tunnels and sewers; an artist's own page says they built "connecting rooms" joining two themes (unverified self-claim). Sources: https://www.gamesradar.com/uk/the-divisions-next-expansions-to-include-randomly-generated-maps-and-survival-gameplay, https://the_preacher.artstation.com/projects/kzAxK
- Seamless worlds: Techland defines seamless as no loading screens, achieved by dividing the city into fragments loaded in full detail around the player (Dying Light 2). Source: https://techland.net/blog/the-development-process-behind-dying-light-2s-environments-and-buildings

**Not found:** how Cyberpunk 2077, Watch Dogs Legion, GTA V storm drains, Saints Row sewers or Dark Souls Depths attach underground to the surface. Nothing on that was returned, so nothing is claimed. OWN knowledge: most of these use hand-made interiors streamed in as separate cells with a door or ladder as the join, which is not the same problem as yours (a pure function of the seed with no stored geometry).

## 5. Building tunnel art cheaply

**Found (thin)**
- Trim sheets pack several tiling strips on one atlas, a mesh is UV-mapped onto it, and decals should be alpha-tested not blended. Source: https://wiki.frozenbyte.com/index.php/3D_Asset_Workflow:_Tile_Textures_and_Trimsheets
- Free tunnel brick PBR sets with grime and moss exist: https://aitextured.com/textures/brick/plaster-brick-01-plaster-rough-old-moss-sewer-tunnel.html. A vertex-colour-driven wall material pack exists for Unreal: https://forums.unrealengine.com/t/procedural-wall-materials/2736333
- Fake light shafts in URP: Shader Graph shaft mesh (https://saifjouda.github.io/SaifJouda/volumetric, https://github.imc.re/Josephy5/FakeVolumetricLight-Unity), additive unlit transparent cubes (https://80.lv/articles/cool-fake-cheap-volumetric-lights-solution-for-unity), mesh stretched toward the light in a shader (https://drcreep.artstation.com/projects/49Y842).
- Flow-map water for the central channel: see L4D2 source in section 3 (2 texture fetches).

**Not found:** sources on spline extrusion of tunnel profiles, world-space/triplanar mapping, triangle counts per ring, fake caustics, or falling-water sheets. OWN knowledge, no source: extrude a cross-section along the path with a ring every 1 to 2 m; a vault arch reads round with 12 to 16 segments per half-circle at this camera distance and brick can come from the texture, not geometry; use world-space (triplanar-lite, top and side only) UVs so adjacent chunks match without UV bookkeeping; drive waterline stain, grime and moss from vertex colour (height above the water plane plus noise); fake caustics by scrolling a caustic texture additively on the ceiling above water; falling water is two crossed or one tilted scrolling-alpha quad plus a splash particle.

## 6. Sound (short)

- Engines express reverb as decay time in seconds, range 0.1 to 20, default about 1.0 to 1.5. Presets named "sewer pipe" exist (Roblox, ZDoom) but no values were found. One blog warns against long decay everywhere because it turns muddy. Sources: https://docs.unity3d.com/460/Documentation/ScriptReference/AudioReverbFilter-decayTime.html, https://create.roblox.com/docs/reference/engine/enums/ReverbType, https://www.zdoom.org/wiki/SNDEAX, https://roblox-studio-sound-service-reverb.pages.dev
- OWN, unsourced: brick tunnel about 1.5 to 2.5 s decay; big cistern 4 to 6 s with a long pre-delay of 40 to 80 ms; the central channel gets a constant water-run loop; random drips as 3D one-shots; muffled street noise via a low-passed loop that fades up near grates; shallow-water footsteps = a splash layer on top of stone steps, triggered by the water-depth state.

## Twelve rules I would follow

1. Make every main channel at least 5 m wide and 4.5 m high, and treat anything narrower as a short crawl. Source: Level Design Book "two player widths minimum" plus Stray's "mitigated by level design", scaled to a 2.6 m character (OWN scaling).
2. Use one fixed door/arch opening size for every kit piece and make it wide enough for the camera. Source: Burgess kit talk (uniform door frame), Gears doors (GamesRadar).
3. Camera: spherecast with a radius, jump in if blocked at once, glide back after a short delay (about 2 to 3 s in Stray), and fade the player if the camera is nearly inside them. Source: Unwound/Stray write-up and free-move-zone article.
4. Never look-up-tilt the camera because the player is against a wall; tilt only on player intent. Source: Stray write-up.
5. Keep a floor of ambient light and an unlit rim or outline so silhouettes always read, then let camp lights carry the contrast. Source: Darkwood (static scenery stays visible), Dying Light 2 "overdid it"; cel outline part is OWN.
6. Enforce a fixed black level for every player, because brightness sliders and gamma tools decide who sees in the dark. Source: Tarkov, Hunt and Amnesia monitor-brightness evidence.
7. Make the flashlight wide and soft rather than a thin beam, and make it a risk (it can attract enemies later). Source: forum "bigger cone less brightness", Dying Light and Alien Isolation flashlight-as-beacon.
8. Drive the water in the central channel with a flow map so it shows the way out or toward the next camp. Source: L4D2 Valve SIGGRAPH slides.
9. Build the maze as a graph first (a big loop plus minor loops and a few dead ends with visible exits on return), then fit tunnels to the graph. Source: Unexplored, Level Design Book dead-end/loop advice.
10. Put a landmark (camp glow, big cistern, grate shaft, waterfall) at every junction cluster, with no plain stretch past about 150 m. Source: landmark guidance (Level Design Book, Unity blog); the 150 m is OWN.
11. Size every kit piece to a multiple of one footprint, keep walls and alcoves inside it, give floors a fixed thickness, and test by looping the kit on itself. Source: Burgess kit talk. This also gives no-overlap generation from the seed.
12. Place rooms at graph nodes with exits as connection points, from hand-made templates carrying randomizer variants, and vary dressing with biome-style rules. Source: Deep Rock Galactic template and exit-node approach; Unexplored node-first resolution.
