# Open space: the city outside its buildings (2026-10-15)

Asked (owner, 2026-10-15 night, with 14 reference images): "Please use these images as inspiration and improve upon the open space pedestrian
walk zones around the buildings. i want my city to feel like it was once alive. ive also included some cool staircases some made into the sides
of mountains that i like. but mainly focus on how we can improve upon the space outside of the buildings. research other video game city
environments see what they do." Then: "not every empty space should be a pedestrian plaza though. there should still be some more empty feeling
areas." What exists today is in `docs/CITY_LIFE.md` (plazas, malls, parking); the layered city (footbridges, stairs, rail) in
`docs/LAYERED_CITY_PLAN.md`; this is the plan for the ground between the buildings.

## 2. What the research says

- **Whyte, The Social Life of Small Urban Spaces (Street Life Project, 1980):** people sit where there are places to sit -- about a linear foot of
  seating per 30 sq ft of plaza (one metre per nine square metres); movable chairs beat fixed benches; the plaza should be hard to tell apart from
  the street (not sunken, not raised); low, easy steps pull people in "almost without a decision"; sun and shade to choose from; trees; water you
  can touch; a food vendor on the corner. Our plazas were built to this in October (`docs/CITY_LIFE.md`); what they lack is the VARIETY and the
  furniture kinds in the images (blocks, sails, tables, lawns, terraces).
- **Project for Public Spaces, the Place Diagram:** a good place is accessible and connected (visible from a distance, active edges, paths where
  people want to go), comfortable (seats, a choice of sun or shade, clean), used (several things to do, used through the day) and sociable.
  Physical advice: see into it from outside; shops not blank walls round it; seats in sun AND shade; a photo opportunity (a focal piece).
- **NYC public plaza zoning (numbers to build to):** seat height 405-510 mm; seat walls at most 455 mm (610 mm within 3 m of the edge); seating
  steps up to 760 mm; seat depth at least 455 mm, a planter ledge used as a seat 560 mm; a seat facing a wall at least 1.8 m from it; a linear
  foot of seating per 30 sq ft.
- **NACTO interim plazas (a street or a leftover corner made a plaza cheaply):** paint or epoxied gravel on the old asphalt, a strong perimeter of
  planters and bollards (Boston asks 90 kg planters and reflective flexible bollards), heavy objects at the corners cars turn round, movable
  tables and chairs, umbrellas; most are rebuilt properly within 3-5 years. In our dead city the interim plaza IS the finished state: the lane
  paint still shows under the tables.
- **Chongqing, Shibati:** 300 m of public stairs over a 40 m drop in 18 flights, lined with houses that hug the cliff; plazas as "city balconies";
  the new hillside districts keep the terrain and stack terraces over 20 m of level difference with winding paths and sunken plazas.
- **San Francisco stairways:** the Hidden Garden Steps (148 steps, 9 flights, tiled risers, planted banks, a name tile) and the 16th Avenue
  Tiled Steps (163 steps) are a straight public stair IN THE LINE OF A STREET too steep to pave, between the house lots, with landings, pipe
  rails and a sign. The same pattern on open slopes is the timber zigzag; on a cliff face the steel switchback on columns.
- **Games.** Cyberpunk 2077 got its "lived-in" feel from density (the wilderness deleted, the content squeezed in) and from VERTICAL reach
  (balconies and fire escapes you can actually get to) -- and its critics' complaint is the lesson: the same handful of clutter everywhere reads
  hollow, so the dressing must change from district to district and tell local stories. Spider-Man's Manhattan gave each neighbourhood to one
  artist who chose everything in it, graffiti to buildings (variety by district, again), and its plazas sit at the foot of landmarks. Cities:
  Skylines' pedestrian areas are promenades: pale pavers, trees in square planters, bollards at the road, and crowds. Our civilians already
  walk the pavements; the plazas should be where they stop.

## 3. What we have (short; the detail is in CITY_LIFE.md)
84 plazas in the 10 km region (small dense blocks, 260 m apart at least), each paved in setts with benches every 7.5 m facing in, bollards along
the kerb, planters at the corners, a dry fountain or a big planter in the middle with four benches, a shuttered kiosk, two heaps of rubble; malls
with painted car parks and lamp posts; parking lots; pavement props every ~36 m (hydrant, bin, cabinet); awnings, canopies, shop signs, roller
shutters, graffiti; entrance ramps and steps; roof billboards; footbridges. No vegetation at all (the lore: nothing has grown since the rapture; the
planters hold dry earth). No pedestrian streets (the old grid city's car-free chunks did not survive the Subversion rewrite). Blocks too thin for
lots are left unpaved (the plan drops a block whose lot line folds: block 55 of seed 58203917 at the classic scale).

## 4. The design

### 4.1 Roles, not a carpet (the owner's second rule)
Every open piece of ground gets a ROLE from its seed, and a real share of them stay empty. The candidates: the plaza blocks, the thin and odd
leftover blocks (median islands), lots that got no building, the setback ground in front of detached buildings, and the leftover triangles at
acute junctions.

| Role | Share of plaza blocks | What is there |
|---|---|---|
| Furnished plaza | 35 % | the kit below in a composed layout: seats, planters, shade, a focal piece, a kiosk |
| Pocket park | 15 % | one or two dry-lawn islands with a kerb, loose chairs (some tipped), a pergola or a round tree bench, a kiosk at the street |
| Promenade | 10 % | a long block: pavers in bands, square planters with dead trees in a row, bollards at the road, benches, murals on the walls facing it |
| Bare yard | 25 % | plain paving (one tone), ONE thing at most: a lone bench, a heap of rubble, a dead tree, a bollard line |
| Vacant lot | 10 % | earth, rubble, a hoarding or fence along the street, nothing else (more at the edge of town, fewer downtown) |
| Quiet court | 5 % | planters only, in a ring or a grid, nothing to sit on |

Districts weigh the roles: downtown more furnished plazas and promenades, old town more quiet courts and pocket parks, outskirts and industrial
more bare yards and vacant lots. A plaza's furniture layout also varies by seed within its role (which focal piece, sails or umbrellas, blocks or
benches), so no two feel alike; the same hash rules as everything else (pure functions of the block index and the world seed).

### 4.2 The kit (new props in `CityProps`, each a few boxes, all in the chunk mesh, no extra draw calls)
- **Seat block** 1.2 x 0.6 x 0.45 m concrete; singles, rows of three, an L round a planter; some with a timber top.
- **Seat wall** 0.45 m high along a planter's edge (the planter ledge deep enough to sit on: 0.56 m).
- **Terraced planters** on a slope: three to five long planters stepping down with seat edges, the steps between them.
- **Shade sail**: three or four steel posts 3.2-4 m, a canted triangle or quad of fabric (a double-sided quad) in rust red, sand or grey; some torn
  (a corner missing), one in five posts standing with no sail.
- **Umbrella table**: a round table 0.8 m, an umbrella 2.4 m across on a pole, three or four chairs, some tipped over; **picnic table** (the parklet).
- **Cube seats** 0.45 m in yellow, red and blue, in threes.
- **Bollard light** 0.9 m with a lit band (the lit-glass face, like the shop signs: glowing at night on a powered block, dark on a dead one).
- **Dead tree**: a bare trunk and six to ten branches, 5-9 m, in a planter or a tree grate (nothing has grown, but what stood there is still
  standing); a **tree planter** 1.2 m square raised, a **round tree bench** round it.
- **Dry-lawn island**: straw-coloured ground inside a curvy 0.12 m kerb (the grass look, dried).
- **Chessboard**: 8 x 8 squares of 0.6 m in two paving tones, two to six pieces 0.9 m high, some fallen.
- **Focal pieces**: the dry fountain (have), a tiered **tower** 12-16 m (five concrete tiers on a core, the Peace Plaza pagoda's shape, one plaza in
  ten), a **pergola** (posts and slats), a boulder **rill** (a dry stone channel) across a promenade.
- **Bike racks** (hoops), a **name wall** lettered in bars, **bins**, a **hoarding** for a vacant lot.

### 4.3 Paving patterns
Three patterns besides the setts: a grid of pale pavers with dark joint bands every 2.4 m, two-tone strips (1.8 m pale, 0.6 m dark), and the
chessboard. Done as geometry (the paving cut in strips with alternating looks in `BuildBlockPaving`), which costs triangles not textures; the look
slices stay the ones we have (Paving, Setts, Asphalt, Earth).

### 4.4 Streets: pedestrian streets, parklets, junction triangles, median islands
- **Pedestrian street**: a short Street-class edge in a dense zone (3-5 % of them by seed; never an arterial, never a bridge or a ramp approach) is
  closed at both ends with planters and bollards, raised to the pavement's height (no kerb, setts or banded pavers), gets no lane paint, and is
  furnished down the middle: tree planters, umbrella tables outside the shops under their awnings, benches, a kiosk. Walkable end to end; the
  civilians keep using it.
- **Parklet**: on a shopping street, a 2 x 6 m platform at the kerb with a planter edge, a picnic table and chairs, one per 150 m of shop frontage.
- **Junction triangle**: a block whose kerb line is a small triangle (under 300 m2) with a road on every side becomes a mini plaza: planters along
  the edges, two umbrella tables, bollards at the points.
- **Median island**: a block too thin for lots (its lot line folds; today dropped unpaved) is paved kerb to kerb and gets a line of planters or dead
  trees, a bench, bollards at the ends. A plan-level change (`CityPlan.Subdivide`: keep the kerb when the lot line fails, mark the block a plaza
  of the "island" role).

### 4.5 Forecourts
A building with a front setback of 3 m or more gets a forecourt between its door and the pavement: where the door's ground is above the pavement,
wide steps with planters inset at the sides and bollard lights down them (the dusk image); where it is level, a strip of pavers with two planters
and a seat block. Downtown towers at the Core get a plaza strip at their foot where the lot allows (plazas at the foot of landmarks, as the games
do).

### 4.6 Hill stairs (the layered plan's L1 "stair streets" and L3)
Where the ground is too steep for a street the plan already bends roads round; the gaps between terraces have nothing in them. Three kinds:
- **Stair street**: a straight public stair in the line of a steep road edge (grade over about 15 %, or two streets at different heights within
  60 m with no road between them): flights of 12-16 risers with landings, a pipe rail down the middle and both sides, risers painted in a colour
  (one in three with a pattern), the banks either side earth and rubble (dead planting), a name tile at the foot, a lamp post at the landings.
- **Zigzag**: on an open slope, timber or steel flights switching back with landings, timber rails.
- **Cliff switchback**: steel flights on concrete columns up a cut face between two terraces, pipe rails.
Each is walkable with real collision, has a way on and off at both ends (a pavement or a plaza), and is tested by a body walking up it.

### 4.7 "Once alive"
The dressing rules: every fountain dry; every planter earth; chairs and tables some tipped; umbrellas some folded, some torn; the kiosk shuttered;
the chessboard with pieces knocked down; rubble in the corners; graffiti on the walls facing the plaza; the lane paint still under the parklet;
dead trees. No litter yet (a litter system is its own thing). And the empty roles above: stillness is part of the mood.

### 4.8 Night
Only the bollard lights and the kiosk's sign band glow (the owner removed street lamps in September: "look bad"); on a dead block nothing does.

## 5. Build order
1. **Roles + kit + paving patterns** on the plaza blocks (the biggest visual change per hour): `World/OpenSpace.cs` (pure: the role and the layout
   from the block index and seed, every prop's rectangle, a walkability check that every prop keeps 1.2 m of clear paving on at least one side and
   the kerb-to-door lines free), `View/CityProps.cs` (the new props), `CitySurfaceBuilder.BuildPlazaFurniture` rewritten over the layout. Tests:
   shares of roles over a region, nothing inside a building or in the road, clearances, every role builds near and far; a PlayMode walk across a
   furnished plaza; `OpenSpaceCapture` renders each role by day and at dusk.
2. **Streets**: pedestrian streets, parklets, junction triangles, median islands (plan change: the island role; new world seed).
3. **Forecourts**.
4. **Hill stairs** (with the layered plan's L1).
Each phase: EditMode + PlayMode, the captures looked at, a new world seed where the plan changed, and a note here.

## 7. Built so far: phase 1, the roles and the kit (2026-10-15 night)

- **`World/OpenSpace.cs`** (pure): every plaza block (Amenities.ChoosePlazas) gets a ROLE from its district and the world seed (`RoleOf`, the
  weights of 4.1 by `ZoneKind`) and a LAYOUT (`LayoutFor`): a list of pieces (`OpenPiece`: kind, where, which way, a capsule footprint, a seed,
  fallen or not, whether a body walks round it) and dry-lawn outlines. Placement rules: every piece keeps `Clearance` 1.2 m of paving from every
  other and `KerbMargin` 1.4 m from the kerb, and keeps off the SPINES -- the straight walks from the middle of every kerb edge to the middle of
  the block (`SpineHalfWidth` 1.2 either side), which stop short of the focal piece -- so a block can be crossed from any side to any other.
  `Walkable` floods a half-metre grid round the pieces from the front kerb's middle and asks for every other kerb's middle and 70 % of the free
  ground; the tests run it over every open block (0 of 59 shut on seed 58203917 at the classic scale). Results on that seed: plaza 23, pocket
  park 10, promenade 0 (the classic blocks are too square), bare yard 12, vacant lot 5, quiet court 9: 44 % empty-feeling.
- **The civic square** (`Plaza`): a focal piece in the middle (the dry fountain, a tiered tower, a pergola or a chessboard with pieces, some
  fallen), seat blocks in rows flanking the walks, shade over the back half (sails on posts, or umbrella tables with chairs), a kiosk and a bike
  rack on the longest kerb, planters at the corners, Whyte's edges (benches facing in every 7.5 m, dead trees in planters every 9 m), bollard
  lights down both sides of every walk, a name wall, bins, rubble. A square wider than `QuadrantExtent` 40 m is composed by QUADRANTS between its
  walks, each a room: a dry lawn with loose chairs and cube seats, a grove of dead trees, a seating court under sails, a terrace of long
  planters with seat blocks, a cafe of umbrella tables; the first is always a court or a cafe (shade), no two neighbours the same.
- **The pocket park**: one or two dry lawns (an ellipse of 12 points pushed in and out, shrunk until it clears the kerb), loose chairs on and
  round them (a third fallen), a pergola or a round tree bench at the back, benches and dead trees along the kerbs, a kiosk. **The promenade**:
  tree planters and benches in two rows every 4.5 m, bins, lights. **The bare yard**: nothing (40 %), or one bench, one heap of rubble or one
  dead tree. **The vacant lot**: earth, 2-4 heaps of rubble, a painted hoarding along the street in two runs with a 3.2 m gap. **The quiet court**:
  planters along the kerbs every 7 m and a sparse grid inside, nothing to sit on.
- **`View/CityProps.OpenSpace.cs`**: the kit as boxes and beams (`AddBeam`: a square beam from a to b at any tilt -- branches, posts, slats,
  fallen pieces): seat block, shade sail (three steel posts, a canted double-sided triangle of fabric, torn = bare posts), umbrella table (table,
  pole, an eight-panel canopy, three or four chairs; fallen = the umbrella down beside it and a chair over), chair (fallen = on its side), picnic
  table, cube seat, bollard light (a band into the lit glass as `SurfaceLook.PackFloodlit` on a powered block, dark otherwise), dead tree (a
  tapering trunk, 4-7 branches going out and up, twigs), tree planter, round tree bench, pergola, tiered tower (five concrete tiers on a stone
  core, a finial), chessboard and pieces, bike rack, name wall (lettering bars), hoarding, bin, lawn kerb. **`View/CitySurfaceBuilder.OpenSpace.cs`**
  draws a block's layout: the paving in the role's look (`OpenSpacePavingLook`: earth for the vacant lot, warm setts for the square and the court,
  plain paving for the rest), the lawns as straw ground 2 cm over the paving with a concrete kerb, the pieces (each by the chunk it stands in), the
  bollards along the kerb. The lit glass reaches the ground pass now (`CitySurfaceBuilder.Build(..., lit)`).
- **Tests**: `OpenSpaceTests` (EditMode: the mix of roles and the empty share, every piece inside its kerb and clear of the others, every block
  crossable on foot from any kerb to any other, the same block the same twice, each role's own things), `OpenSpaceWalkPlayTests` (a body the
  player's size walks the front walk of the nearest square to the middle and the side walk out, on the streamed paving among the real pieces),
  `OpenSpaceCapture` (Explicit, -Graphics: one block of each role from above by day and at dusk, and from a person's eye at its kerb,
  `Logs/CityShots/open-*.png`).
- **Decided without asking** (the questions in 6): dead trees yes (bare, in planters and groves: what stood, stands); lawns as dry straw; colour
  on the umbrellas, sails and cube seats (rust, sand, slate, bottle green, faded blue; yellow, red, blue cubes); the shares of 4.1.
- **Not yet** (phase 2 on): the paving patterns of 4.3 (new texture slices), the streets of 4.4 (pedestrian streets, parklets, junction
  triangles, median islands), forecourts, hill stairs, the night glow checked up close, civilians stopping in the squares.

## 8. Phase 2, planned: the streets (how, in this code)

- **Pedestrian streets.** A pure rule, no plan storage: `PedestrianStreets.Is(plan, RoadEdge)` -- a Street-class edge, not a bridge, 30-120 m long,
  in dense country (population over 0.5 at its middle), both ends at nodes with three or more live edges (closing it cuts nothing off), chosen
  3-5 % by a hash of the edge id and the world seed. Drawn by `CitySurfaceBuilder`: the ribbon (`BuildRoad`) at the PAVEMENT's height in setts
  with no lane paint (`BuildPaint` skips it); the block edge along it draws no kerb face (`BuildPavement` finds the road edge of a block edge by
  its two node positions: `PlanWorld.EdgeBetween`); bollards and planters across both ends just inside the trims; furniture down the middle
  (tree planters every 9 m alternating with umbrella tables and benches on the centre line, 3.5 m of walk either side). The junction plates at
  the ends stay asphalt.
- **Parklets.** On a street with shopfronts (the facing building's `WindowPlan.Storefront`), one per 150 m of frontage by hash: a 2 x 6 m
  platform at the kerb, at pavement height, inside the ribbon, with a planter edge, a picnic table and two chairs; the kerb face is interrupted
  by it. Drawn with the block edge's pavement.
- **Leftover blocks and median islands.** Every paved block with no lots (a tiny junction triangle; a block too thin for lots, which the plan
  will PAVE instead of dropping once `CityPlan.Subdivide` keeps the kerb line when the lot line fails) is an open block too: `OpenSpace.LayoutFor`
  takes them (`IsOpen`: paved, no mall, no lot cut from it) with roles by size -- under 300 m2 a junction triangle (planters along the kerbs, two
  umbrella tables, bollards at the points), a thin one a median island (a line of planters and dead trees, a bench, bollards at the ends),
  otherwise the roles of 4.1. A plan change: a new world seed with it.
- **Paving patterns (4.3).** Three new texture slices in `FacadeTextures` (Rows 3 -> 4, slices 12-14): "pavers" (1.2 m pale pavers, a dark joint
  band every 2.4 m), "strips" (1.8 m pale and 0.6 m dark across) and "drygrass" (straw); `SurfaceLook.Pavers/Strips/DryGrass`; the scene builder
  redraws the atlas when its size changes. The square and the promenade use pavers and strips by seed; the lawns use the grass slice instead
  of tinted earth.
- Tests as in phase 1: the rule's shares, the geometry (no piece in the road, the walks clear), a body walking a pedestrian street end to end,
  captures. Then phase 3 forecourts, phase 4 hill stairs.
